# Interview 01 — Maria, Shift Manager (restaurant group, 3 locations)

> Fictional transcript for training. Maria manages front-of-house scheduling for a 3-location casual restaurant group, ~70 staff total. She signed up for a ShiftLoop trial, abandoned it the first day, and came back a week later. Lightly edited for length; the messiness, tangents, and contradictions are intentional.

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time. Take me back to the first time you sat down with ShiftLoop. What were you trying to do?

**Maria:** Honestly? I was trying to get out the door. It was a Thursday, I had to post the next week's schedule, and our old way is this giant Google Sheet that I've been copying forward for like three years. Someone told me ShiftLoop would do it automatically. So I signed up between the lunch and dinner rush, which — mistake number one, never do anything important between lunch and dinner.

**Interviewer:** What happened?

**Maria:** So it asks me to "add my team." Fine. But it's one person at a time. Name, role, phone, hourly rate, availability... times seventy people. I did maybe six and went, no. Absolutely not. I have all of this *already*, it's in my sheet, why am I retyping it? I closed the tab. I went back to the Google Sheet that night like I always do.

**Interviewer:** But you came back.

**Maria:** I came back because my district manager kept pushing it. Second time I found there's an import thing — you can upload a spreadsheet. Which, great, that's what I wanted! But my columns didn't match whatever it wanted, and it kept rejecting the file, and there was no... it just said "invalid format." It didn't tell me *what* was invalid. I literally guessed at the column names for like twenty minutes. Eventually I got it. But I'm stubborn. A normal person quits there.

**Interviewer:** Once your team was in, what then?

**Maria:** Then it offered to just *make* the schedule for me. The auto thing. And I'll be honest, I didn't trust it. I don't trust a robot to know that Tony physically cannot close on Wednesdays because he's got his kid, or that you never, ever put Sara and Marcus on the same shift. That stuff isn't in any system, it's in my head. So it made me a schedule and I basically threw it out and did it by hand anyway. Felt like it wasted my time honestly.

**Interviewer:** So the auto-schedule wasn't useful?

**Maria:** I mean... the *bones* were okay? Coverage was right, it didn't double-book anyone. But I couldn't see *why* it put people where it did, so I didn't believe it. If I can't see the reasoning I'm not going to stake Friday night dinner service on it. Maybe if I'd used it longer. But who has time to learn to trust something when you're slammed?

**Interviewer:** You mentioned your old Google Sheet a few times — fondly almost.

**Maria:** Ha. Not fondly. It's a disaster. But it's *my* disaster, you know? I know where everything is. Switching costs me time *now* to maybe save time *later*, and "later" is a luxury I don't have on a Thursday afternoon. That's the whole thing with us. We're not anti-software. We're anti- *anything that costs me twenty minutes I don't have today.*

**Interviewer:** If you could've changed one thing about that first session?

**Maria:** Let me bring my sheet in cleanly and be scheduling in five minutes. That's it. The auto-magic, the phone app, all that — I didn't care yet. I just needed to not start from a blank screen typing names. Get me to a published schedule once and I'll probably never leave. But that first twenty minutes almost lost me twice.

**Interviewer:** Did your staff ever see it? The phone side?

**Maria:** Eventually, yeah, and that part was fine, no complaints. But that's later. By the time staff see anything I've already had to do all the painful part. The pain is all upfront, before I've gotten anything out of it.
