# Interview 03 — Priya, Store Manager (specialty retail, single location)

> Fictional transcript for training. Priya manages a single specialty-retail store, ~18 staff, high proportion of part-time students. She activated quickly and is a fan — but her pain points are different, and she contradicts Devon on the staff app and the auto-scheduler. The contradictions and the off-topic tangent (tips/pay visibility) are deliberate.

**Interviewer:** You got up and running pretty fast, I hear. Walk me through it.

**Priya:** Yeah! Setup was honestly fine for me. But I think that's because I only have eighteen people, and half of them are students who turn over every semester anyway, so I just typed them in. Took maybe fifteen minutes. If I had eighty people I'd probably have screamed. So I don't want to oversell it — I was an easy case.

**Interviewer:** And the auto-scheduler?

**Priya:** Love it. Genuinely. It's the reason I stayed. My availability situation is *chaos* — students changing their hours every two weeks around classes — and the auto-scheduler just slots everyone in around it and I tweak a couple things. Before, I was doing a logic puzzle every Sunday night. Now it's fifteen minutes. I tell other managers, just let it do the first draft, you can always fix it.

**Interviewer:** Some managers say they don't trust it.

**Priya:** I mean, I get it, I was nervous the first time too. But you publish one schedule it made and nothing catches fire, and you relax. The trust thing fixes itself if you actually try it once. The problem is people don't get *to* the trying-it part, right? They quit before they ever see it work.

**Interviewer:** So if setup wasn't your pain, what was?

**Priya:** Getting availability *out of my staff.* That was — that *is* — the nightmare. The tool wants everyone's availability, which, fair, it needs it. But my staff don't log in and enter it. I'm chasing eighteen college kids over text: "what are your hours next week," screenshots, one girl literally sends me a photo of her class schedule written on a napkin. So I'm the bottleneck, I'm transcribing all of it into ShiftLoop myself. The auto-scheduler is only as good as the availability I feed it, and feeding it is all manual chasing.

**Interviewer:** Does the staff phone app help with that at all?

**Priya:** The app's great, actually — and here's where I'll disagree with whoever told you staff won't use it. My staff *live* on their phones. They check their shifts constantly, they do swaps with each other, it cut my "when do I work" texts to almost zero. So the *viewing* side, they adopted instantly. It's the *entering availability* side they won't do, even in the same app. They'll happily check a shift, they won't proactively go enter their availability for next month. Same app, totally different behavior.

**Interviewer:** Why do you think that is?

**Priya:** Checking your shift has an immediate payoff — you find out when you work. Entering availability is a chore with no payoff for *them*, the payoff is all mine. So they don't. If the app nudged them — "hey, enter next week or you might not get scheduled" — maybe. Right now it just sits there waiting and they ignore it.

**Interviewer:** Anything else on your wishlist, while I've got you?

**Priya:** Ooh — kind of off-topic, but my staff keep asking if they can see their tips and their pay stuff in the app. Right now that's a totally separate system. It comes up constantly. I know that's probably not what you're building but you asked. [laughs] Other than that, no — honestly once I was set up I was happy. My whole struggle was the chasing-availability thing, not the tool itself.
